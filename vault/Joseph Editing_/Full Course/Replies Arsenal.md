Section 1: Objection Handling Principles

Before diving into the replies, understand this:

Objections are not rejection.

They are signals of uncertainty, misalignment, or lack of information.

Your goal is not to “win” the argument.

Your goal is to move the prospect one step closer to clarity.

Every effective reply does one of three things:

1. Reframes the objection

2. Qualifies the prospect

3. Advances toward a conversation

If a reply does none of these, it’s noise.

Section 2: Core Objection Reply Templates

Objection 1: “What’s the cost?”

Objective: Reframe price → results, and filter out price-shoppers without sounding arrogant. Reply Template:

Hey {{Name}},

Totally fair question. You probably get a lot of emails like this every day, so you need a way to filter through what’s worth your time.

Let me ask you this - what’s more important to you right now:

Getting the cheapest option, or getting real results?

If you’re purely looking for the lowest price, I’m probably not the right fit—and that’s completely okay.

Objection 2: “No, thank you.”

Objective: Clarify why they’re saying no instead of accepting a vague rejection. Reply Template:

Got it, no worries at all.

Before I let you go, can I ask you one last thing?

Is it a “not interested” because you’re not looking to buy anything right now?

Or is it a “not interested” because you’re already fully booked and couldn’t handle additional clients even if it worked?

Why this works:

● Polite, respectful, and low pressure

● Turns a dead end into a diagnostic moment

● Often reopens conversations that would otherwise die

Objection 6: “Can you just send me the price?”

Objective: Protect customization and avoid premature quoting.

Reply Option A (Soft):

Totally fair - the reason I can’t send a price right now is because we don’t sell one-size-fits-all packages.

We have multiple options depending on where you’re currently at, and I don’t want to recommend something you don’t need.

Would you be against a quick conversation to see if this would even make sense first?

Reply Option B (Firm):

I honestly can’t, and that’s not me being difficult.

Our pricing depends heavily on your current situation, goals, and constraints. It would be impossible to give an accurate breakdown over email.

More importantly, we don’t make offers to everyone. We only move forward if we’re confident we can keep your marketing costs under control and actually deliver results.

If it’s cool with you, I’d rather touch base when you have a bit more time to see if this even makes sense at all.

Why this works:

● Protects perceived value

● Frames selectivity as professionalism

● Keeps control of the sales process

Objection 8: “We’re already working on it.”

Objective: Respect existing efforts while positioning your solution as additive or clarifying. Reply Template:

Got it, and I’m not looking to take anything away from what you already have going on.

That said, I don’t want to jump the gun or offer something you don’t need.

Would you be against a quick conversation just to see if this would even make sense alongside what you’re already doing?

Why this works:

● Removes threat perception

● Positions you as consultative, not competitive

● Keeps door open without pressure
