The X Protocol

X (formerly Twitter) is not the primary client acquisition channel we rely on, that role belongs to cold email. Email gives you control over volume, targeting, and differentiation. X does not.

However, dismissing X entirely would be a mistake.

Despite its flaws, X has consistently produced some of the highest-paying editing clients I’ve ever worked with. When used correctly, it functions as a secondary acquisition engine and a long-term personal brand asset that compounds over time.

This module outlines a precise, repeatable protocol for using X to:

● Build inbound demand from creators actively looking for editors

● Identify and convert high-intent prospects through outbound signals ● Manage DMs in a way that leads to paid work, not endless conversations

X is not about volume.

It is about visibility, positioning, and consistency.

Why We Use X (and Its Limitations)

X is the central hub where:

● YouTubers

● Content creators

● Editors

all coexist in the same ecosystem.

This makes it uniquely valuable, but also uniquely competitive.

Your main disadvantage on X is saturation. When a creator posts publicly that they need an editor, they are immediately flooded with hundreds of DMs, many from editors who will:

● Underprice you

● Potentially be as good as or better than you

This is why X should never replace cold outreach.

Instead, we use it for two specific purposes:

1. Inbound demand through proof of work

2. Signal-based outbound to the most aware prospects

The Two Sides of X

There are only two strategies that matter on X:

1. Inbound: Clients come to you

2. Outbound: You go to clients

Let’s start with inbound.

The Inbound Protocol

Inbound on X is built entirely on proof of work.

If a creator is hiring an editor, they are looking for one thing above all else: Evidence that you can edit at a high level.

Prerequisite: Skill Threshold

Before attempting this strategy, you must be able to objectively say:

“My editing looks aesthetic and professional.”

This does not require elite mastery, but it does require competence.

You should have:

● At least one month of consistent editing practice

● Work you are genuinely proud of

If you cannot produce clean, aesthetic edits yet, stop here and practice.

The Weekly Preview System

Every single week, without fail, you will post one preview.

What Is a Preview?

A preview is:

● ~30 seconds total

● A compilation of your best edited moments

How to Create One

1. Review all edits from the past week

○ Client work or practice edits

2. Identify:

○ Animations

○ Effects

○ Moments you’re proud of

3. Select 3-5 clips

4. Stitch them into one 30-second video

Posting Guidelines

● Post once per week

● Same day every week (consistency matters more than timing)

Caption Template

Keep it simple and keyword-friendly:

Intro for @CreatorName

Edited on [Software]

That’s it.

The Outbound Role of X

Inbound builds authority.

Outbound creates momentum.

While inbound previews compound over time, outbound on X allows you to tap into immediate demand. The mistake most editors make is treating outbound like cold outreach. It isn’t.

Outbound on X is signal-based, not interruptive.

We are not convincing people they need an editor.

We are responding to people who have already stated they do.

Signal-Based Outreach

Signal-based outreach means only contacting prospects who have explicitly declared intent. On X, this intent looks like:

● “Looking for a video editor”

● “Need an editor ASAP”

● “Hiring an editor for my channel”

These are the most valuable prospects you can reach:

● Problem-aware

● Solution-aware

● Ready to buy

Your job is not to persuade.

Your job is to prove competence and respond professionally.

The Daily Search Protocol

Step 1: Search Queries

Use X’s search bar and type:

● “need a video editor”

● “looking for a video editor”

Then switch to the Latest tab.

You will see:

● Editors posting (ignore them)

● Creators posting (these are your targets)

Scroll until you find a creator or YouTuber explicitly stating they need an editor.

The Appointment-Setting Framework

Once inbound and outbound are running together, DMs will stack quickly.

Your job is not to chat.

Your job is to control the conversation.

The Threading Rule

Every message you send must end with a question.

This is non-negotiable.

This technique is called threading:

● You respond

● You ask a question

● They respond

● You ask another question

If you do not do this, you will get ghosted.

The SDP Framework

(Style → Deadline → Price)

Because these prospects are already product-aware, you do not need a full discovery call. Use the SDP framework instead.

1. Style

First question:

“What style are you looking for?”

This forces them to:

● Send references

● Clarify expectations

2. Deadline

Second question:

“What’s the turnaround time you need on these videos?”

This establishes:

● Urgency

● Workload

● Operational pressure

You are now defining the gap between their current state and desired outcome.

3. Price

Now, and only now, you give the price.

Structure:

“For [style] videos with a [deadline] turnaround, the price would be $X.” Do not:

● Ask for their budget

● Give a range

● Apologize

Give the price cleanly.

Confidence here is not optional.

Price Objections

Price resistance is always one of two things:

1. Logistical - They genuinely don’t have the money

2. Value-based - They don’t see the ROI

Your job is to identify which one it is.

If It’s Value-Based

Ask:

● What are you doing for editing right now?

● How often are you uploading?

● What’s the cost of delays or missed uploads?

Use their answers to re-anchor value against your price.

If It’s Logistical

Do not immediately drop the price.

Instead, remove value.

Example:

“I could do $200 per video, but turnaround would be slower and footage would need to be sent by Monday.”

This preserves:

● Your original pricing integrity

● The perceived value of your main offer

Never barge downward without trade-offs.
