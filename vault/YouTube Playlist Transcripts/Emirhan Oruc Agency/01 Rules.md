# 01 Rules

## Practical rules implied across the 10 transcripts

1. **Do not pick a niche that cannot easily pay $3k+/month.**
   - Low-ticket, low-margin businesses are a bad fit for fast profitable scaling.

2. **Do not stay broad when you can sub-niche.**
   - “Coaches” is weak.
   - “Financial coaches for expats” / “pre-postnatal fitness coaches” style specificity is stronger.

3. **Do not sell editing alone if you want higher retainers.**
   - Add the pieces that create measurable business results.

4. **Tie your offer to appointments, pipeline, or cash outcomes.**
   - The more direct the business impact, the easier it is to charge and retain.

5. **Do not confuse activity with leverage.**
   - 400 bad looms with 0 booked calls is worse than 100 well-positioned looms with a client signed.

6. **Fix the message before increasing volume.**
   - Market, offer, and angle first; scale volume second.

7. **Use personalization where it matters.**
   - Personalized Looms / direct examples beat generic spam when the offer is strong.

8. **Follow up more and across more touchpoints.**
   - Being seen once on one platform is usually not enough.
   - Email + IG + follow-up + other channels are repeatedly framed as stronger than single-channel outreach.

9. **Book calls proactively; do not wait for inbound hand-raisers only.**
   - If the audience is already there, message followers/leads directly.

10. **Keep founder time on sales and high-level decisions as long as possible.**
    - But remove repetitive pieces: list work, first-touch outreach, follow-ups, appointment setting.

11. **Delegate before burnout forces you to.**
    - If you are doing fulfillment, sales admin, outreach, and team management yourself, you will stall.

12. **Build proof into the operating system.**
    - Every client win should become a testimonial, case study, content asset, and sales weapon.

13. **Do not keep bad-fit clients just because they pay.**
    - Some transcripts explicitly frame firing wrong clients as necessary to rebuild better.

14. **Retention comes from innovation and client care, not just deliverables.**
    - Keep improving systems and helping clients solve the next bottleneck.

15. **Think in systems, not hacks.**
    - The recurring theme is infrastructure: offer, acquisition, fulfillment, delegation, proof flywheel.
